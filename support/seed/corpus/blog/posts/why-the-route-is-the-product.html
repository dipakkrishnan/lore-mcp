Why the route is the product

Batching a groomer's day by neighbourhood saves about an hour of driving; that hour is the whole pitch.

When Delia first booked customers in the order they called, her van crossed Port Alder four times a day. Tidewell now sorts the day into clusters, and three small rules add up to the hour:

  • Stops in the same neighbourhood are offered together.
  • The first slot of the day is the one closest to the van's home.
  • A cancellation pulls a nearby booking forward instead of leaving a gap.
The route is the product. The booking form is just how it gets fed.
